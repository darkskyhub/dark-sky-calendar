Times are computed for Coonabarabran / Siding Spring Observatory (SSO), latitude 31.27 south, longitude 149.06 east. Timezone Australia/Sydney. Early night means sunset until four hours after sunset. That is the window most guided night tours actually use.

Dark Night

A Dark Night here means the early evening is actually dark: from 3 days after Full Moon through to 3 days after New Moon. The three days either side of Full Moon are poor for the Milky Way and faint objects.

Early night planets

Only planets that stand at least about 10 degrees above the horizon at some point between sunset and four hours after sunset. Morning-only planets are not listed.

  • Mars. North-east later in the evening.

  • Jupiter. North-east later in the evening.

  • Saturn. West after sunset.

Worth looking at

Short list for the same early-night window. Objects operators actually point at, not a catalogue dump.

  1. 1. Southern Cross and Pointers

    South-east through the early night.

    Naked eye.

  2. 2. Orion Nebula

    North through the early night.

    Binoculars or a small telescope help.

  3. 3. Omega Centauri

    South-east later in the evening.

    A glow to the naked eye. Binoculars help.

  4. 4. Carina and Eta Carinae

    South-east through the early night.

    Naked eye. Binoculars help with the nebula.

  5. 5. Jewel Box

    South-east later in the evening.

    Binoculars or a small telescope help.

  6. 6. Tarantula and the Large Magellanic Cloud

    South through the early night.

    Naked eye. Binoculars help with the Tarantula.
